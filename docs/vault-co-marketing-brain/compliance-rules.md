# Compliance Rules — Vault Co AI Campaign Builder

What the AI must never generate. These rules apply to all campaign drafts, ad copy, lead form language, SMS content, and GHL workflow scripts.

## Meta Ad Policy Violations

### False or Unverifiable Claims

**Never say:**
- "Best roofer in [City]" — unverifiable superlative
- "Cheapest prices in [Area]" — unverifiable and contradicts premium positioning
- "#1 roofing company" — unless independently verified with citation
- "Guaranteed lowest price" — unverifiable and misleading

**Why it matters:** Meta will reject or limit reach on ads containing unverifiable superlatives. They also erode trust with buyers who recognize marketing inflation.

### Guaranteed Insurance Outcomes

**Never say:**
- "Insurance will cover it"
- "Get a free roof through your insurance"
- "100% covered by your homeowner's insurance"
- "File a claim and pay nothing"
- "Guaranteed claim approval"
- "We'll get your insurance to pay for your roof"
- "Your roof replacement is covered — just call us"

**Why it matters:** These statements are factually false (insurance approval depends on the policy, adjuster, and damage assessment), constitute misrepresentation, and create legal exposure for the client. Meta has specific policies against misleading insurance claim language in home improvement ads.

**Safe alternative:** "We'll assess your roof and help you understand your options, including whether an insurance claim may apply."

### Guaranteed Results

**Never say:**
- "Guaranteed X leads per month"
- "We guarantee bookings"
- "Guaranteed ROI"
- "You will get [specific outcome]"

**Why it matters:** No outcome in advertising can be guaranteed. Any guarantee language creates advertiser liability and is disallowed by Meta.

### Urgency That Cannot Be Enforced

**Never say:**
- "Only 5 spots left" (unless spots are genuinely tracked and limited)
- "Offer expires tonight" (unless a real expiration exists)
- "Price goes up tomorrow" (unless provably true)
- "Last chance" (unless it is genuinely the last chance)

**Safe urgency:** "Limited inspection slots available this week" is acceptable only if the schedule is actually limited. Real scarcity is fine. Manufactured scarcity is deceptive.

### Discriminatory Targeting Language

**Never include in copy or targeting:**
- Audience exclusions or inclusions based on race, religion, national origin, gender, disability, or familial status when running housing-adjacent campaigns
- Language that implies the offer is only for certain demographic groups
- Neighborhood targeting that effectively excludes protected classes

**Why it matters:** Meta's Special Ad Category rules apply to housing-related campaigns in the United States. Violation can result in ad account suspension. Target by behavior, income range, homeownership status, and home age — not by demographic identity.

## SMS and TCPA Compliance

### Consent Requirements

Every SMS sequence must have explicit written consent captured before the first message is sent.

**Required in all lead forms:**
- A visible consent checkbox or disclosure statement
- Clear statement that the lead is consenting to receive SMS messages from [Company Name]
- Phone number identified as the number that will receive messages
- STOP opt-out instruction included in consent language and in every promotional SMS

**TCPA consent language (minimum):**
> "By submitting this form, I agree to receive calls and text messages from [Company Name] at the number provided, including by automated means. Message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP to unsubscribe."

### Prohibited SMS Practices

- Sending SMS without consent captured in the lead form
- Continuing automated sequences after a STOP reply
- Sending more than 3 messages per day to any single contact
- Using misleading sender identification
- Sending bulk promotional messages to purchased lists

## Storm Damage and Insurance Advertising

This is the highest-risk area in roofing advertising and requires specific attention.

### What is Allowed

- Offering a free storm damage assessment
- Explaining that the company will help the homeowner understand their options
- Describing storm damage inspection services
- Mentioning that some repairs may be covered by homeowner's insurance (without guarantee)
- Urgency based on real storm events (with compliance review)

### What is Not Allowed

- Claiming insurance will cover the repair
- Implying the roof will be free or zero-cost
- Describing how to "work the system" with insurance
- Creating false urgency around claim filing deadlines that do not exist
- Targeting homeowners in areas where no storm occurred

### State-Specific Considerations

Some states (Texas, Colorado, Florida, others) have specific contractor laws around insurance claim assistance, assignment of benefits, and contractor solicitation after storm events. Campaigns targeting these markets should note this in the compliance section of the draft and recommend attorney review before launch.

## Fear-Based Advertising

### Aggressive Fear Tactics — Not Allowed

- Showing graphic structural damage with copy designed to frighten homeowners
- Exaggerating the consequences of inaction in a way that is not proportional to real risk
- Implying that a homeowner's family is in immediate danger without factual basis
- Using language designed to panic rather than inform

### Honest Urgency — Allowed

- Explaining that small damage becomes large damage over time (factually accurate)
- Noting that winter conditions can worsen unaddressed damage (factually accurate)
- Explaining that manufacturer warranties may have inspection requirements (factually accurate)

The distinction: Is the claim factually supportable and proportionate? If yes, it is honest urgency. If the copy is designed to override the homeowner's judgment through fear rather than inform their decision, it is aggressive fear-based advertising.

## Brand-Specific Compliance Rules

### Premium-Positioned Clients

If the client is positioned as a premium or quality-first provider, never use:
- "cheapest" or "lowest price"
- Discount-first language
- Competing on price

Premium positioning is destroyed by price-first copy, even if the price is competitive.

### Before/After Creative

- Use only authentic client work
- Do not use competitor jobs or sourced photos
- Do not use AI-generated or stock transformation imagery
- Do not imply dramatic transformations that are not typical of the client's work

## Approval Warnings Required

The following items must always be flagged for human review before campaign launch:

1. Any copy mentioning insurance, claims, or coverage
2. Any urgency claims (verify they are enforceable)
3. Any before/after creative (verify it is authentic client work)
4. Any specific pricing, discounts, or guarantees
5. Any testimonials (verify they are real, consented, and not embellished)
6. Campaigns targeting recent storm areas (verify storm actually occurred)
7. Any campaigns in states with special contractor solicitation laws
8. SMS workflows (verify TCPA consent is captured in the lead form)
